# General SOP for Building a Good Game

## 1. Define the Fantasy

Start with the experience you want the player to have.

Ask:

> What does the player get to be?

Examples:

- A survivor
- A criminal mastermind
- A football manager
- A shop owner
- A detective

Don't start with features. Start with the experience.

---

## 2. Define the Core Loop

Describe the game in one sentence:

> Player does X → gets Y → makes decision Z → repeat

If you can't describe the loop simply, the game probably isn't designed tightly enough yet.

---

## 3. Identify the Primary Decision

Ask:

> What is the player repeatedly deciding?

Examples:

- Attack or defend
- Explore or return
- Spend or save
- Risk or play safe
- Fight or flee
- Upgrade A or B

This is usually the heart of the game.

---

## 4. Build the Smallest Possible Prototype

Don't build the actual game yet.

Build something that answers:

> Is the core decision fun?

Use placeholders, basic UI, ugly graphics, whatever works.

Ideally this takes hours or a couple of days, not weeks.

---

## 5. Play It Yourself Repeatedly

Don't ask:

> Is this a good idea?

Ask:

- Do I want another round?
- Where did I get bored?
- Where did I feel clever?
- Where did I feel frustrated?
- Did I understand why I won/lost?
- Was there a memorable moment?

---

## 6. Add Depth, Not Complexity

Once the basic loop works, add systems that create new decisions.

Good complexity:

> New mechanic → creates new strategic possibilities

Bad complexity:

> New mechanic → gives the player another button/menu/stat to manage

A useful rule:

> Every system should interact with another system.

That's how emergent gameplay appears.

---

## 7. Add Progression

Give the player a reason to come back.

Possible progression:

- New abilities
- New characters
- New levels
- Unlocks
- Harder difficulties
- Cosmetics
- Rankings
- Achievements
- Story

Progression should support the core game rather than compensate for a boring loop.

---

## 8. Add Variability

Replayability often comes from combinations rather than simply adding more content.

For example:

> Random map + random enemies + different starting abilities + player choices

can create hundreds of situations from relatively little content.

---

## 9. Make Failure Interesting

A good game doesn't necessarily need to make winning amazing.

It needs to make losing tolerable or interesting enough that the player wants another attempt.

Ideally, the player thinks:

> Damn. One more try.

---

## 10. Playtest With Strangers

Give the game to people who didn't build it.

Don't explain anything unless absolutely necessary.

Watch what they do.

Look for:

- Confusing mechanics
- Exploits
- Boring sections
- Unexpected strategies
- Things they love that you didn't expect

Real player behavior is more valuable than assumptions.

---

## 11. Iterate Ruthlessly

Use this loop:

> Build → Play → Observe → Change → Repeat

Don't become attached to mechanics.

If something isn't working:

> Remove it.

Don't keep a mechanic just because you spent three days building it.

---

## 12. Polish Last

Only after the game is fun, focus heavily on:

- Art
- Animations
- Sound
- Music
- UI
- Effects
- Optimization
- Tutorials
- Accessibility
- Monetization

Polish makes a good game better.

It rarely makes a bad game fun.

---
